The model

One job moves along one line. Each step has one owner.

Before, the user carried a job by hand from Jobs to Resume to Ready to apply to Applications. Now a job is picked once and the agents carry it. The user is asked only where a decision is really theirs: which job, whether the new details in the resume are true, and the final Submit.

You a decision that is the user'sAgent work done without being askedScreen where it happens
Navigation

From a row of tools to five places

No page is deleted. "Ready to apply" becomes the Getting ready view of Applications, Profile moves beside Settings, and Today is new: it is the home and the place where the agents report.

Onboarding

Resume first. The form fills itself.

The server already reads a resume before any other answer and returns suggested titles, level and skills. So the questions become a confirmation, and the long form is no longer the price of the first result.

Before

Situation→Basics→Goal→Preferences→Resume→Matching→Confirm→Tour

Six to eight screens. The resume, which answers most of the questions, is asked for fifth. The first job card appears after the tour.

After

1 · Your resume→2 · Is this what you're looking for?→Matching, on the same screen→Today

The second screen arrives filled in: titles, level, where, kind of work, how soon. One button, "Find my jobs". Without a resume the same screen starts empty.

POST /v2/resumes/upload the file, parsed in the request POST /onboarding/resume → suggested titles, level, skills PUT /onboarding/steps/situation PUT /onboarding/steps/basics PUT /onboarding/steps/resume POST /onboarding/match real phases, streamed POST /onboarding/confirm POST /onboarding/complete → /today

The server is unchanged. The stage machine stays the authority; the web saves the same steps in the same order with fewer screens.

Nothing is lost. Career goal, industries, company size, pay floor and the LinkedIn URL are asked later, where they are used: the search panel and the profile.

Who keeps the long flow. A user who left early and comes back mid-way, and GoApply, whose consent and identity steps are legal requirements.

As built

The screens, from the branch

Seven use cases

Each flow, step by step

Steps are numbered because the order is the point. Each step shows who acts. The highlighted step is always the user's own Submit.

1. New, has a resume, needs a job now

Maya was laid off last week. She has a resume and no patience for a form.
  1. YouSign upEmail or Google.
  2. YouDrop the resumeUpload, paste, or a LinkedIn PDF.
  3. AgentReads itSuggests titles, level and skills.
  4. YouConfirm the goalChange any chip. "Find my jobs".
  5. AgentSearches and comparesThe real phases show while it runs.
  6. TodayThree best fitsThe gap leads each one.
  7. YouPrepare applicationOne tap, the cost is shown, confirm.
  8. AgentTailors and writesResume, letter if asked, answers.
  9. YouVerify detailsKeep, remove or rewrite each new claim.
  10. YouOpen applicationThe extension fills the form.
  11. YouClick SubmitOn the employer's site. Always the user.
  12. AgentTracks itApplied, then a follow-up after 10 days.

Checked: the user types nothing between the resume and the first job unless a suggestion is wrong.

Still slow: step 5 has a server target of 45 seconds and a cap of two minutes. See finding 2.

2. New, no resume yet

Jun is a final-year student with nothing written down.
  1. You"I don't have one yet"The goal screen opens empty.
  2. YouPick titles and whereThe same chips, typed by hand.
  3. AgentSearches from the answersNo fit score: there is no resume to compare.
  4. Today"Add a resume" waitsIt is the primary button.
  5. YouBuild or uploadThe guided builder in Resume.
  6. AgentScores appearPicks now carry gap lines.

Checked: jobs without a resume never show a number called a fit.

3. The daily two minutes

Maya, day six. She opens the app on the train.
  1. Today"3 things are waiting"The home says it before anything else.
  2. YouCheck and openThe kit that finished overnight.
  3. YouClick SubmitAfter the extension fills the form.
  4. YouSee the quiet ones3 with no reply in 10 days; a follow-up is drafted.
  5. YouPrepare one moreFrom the picks, without leaving Today.
  6. Today"Nothing is waiting"A clear end to the session.

Checked: every step starts on one screen. Before, this was Ready to apply, then Applications, then Jobs.

4. "I want these companies"

Dev wants Stripe, Linear or Figma and nothing else.
  1. YouChange the goalFrom the goal line on Today.
  2. YouName the companiesOnly these, or prefer these.
  3. AgentRanks them firstThe saved search already supports it.
  4. YouPrepare applicationThe resume is tailored to that posting.

Gap found: a company with no open posting cannot be targeted, and tailoring is per job, not per company. See finding 7.

5. "Why am I not getting interviews?"

Priya has applied to forty jobs with one resume.
  1. TodayResume laneWhat postings ask for that the resume never mentions.
  2. YouImprove my resumeOpens the resume hub on AI tailoring.
  3. AgentTailors a versionFit before and after, side by side.
  4. YouVerify detailsNothing new is kept without an OK.
  5. AgentUses itAttached when that job is prepared.

Checked: the gap is stated as evidence ("it is not on your resume"), never as a verdict on the person.

6. When something goes wrong

Each state says what happened, what was spent, and what to do next.
  1. CreditsOut of kitsThe cost sheet says how many can be prepared now; nothing starts.
  2. AI offNo AI for the accountKits use the resume as it is, and say so.
  3. ClosedThe posting is gone"Nothing was prepared." No credit is used.
  4. EmptyNothing fitsThe lane says how many were read, then offers Change.
  5. FailedA lane does not loadThat lane offers Retry; the others keep working.

7. On a phone

The same order in one column.
  1. BarFive slotsToday · Jobs · Applications · Resume · More.
  2. TodayWaiting firstThe primary button is the first thing under the title.
  3. JobsOne search lineThe first job card is on the first screen.
  4. Targets44 px and upEvery control; no sideways scroll at 375 px.

Trade-off: Interview prep moves into More on phones to make room for Today. See finding 9.

Review findings

What walking the flows turned up

Each flow was walked against the code. Some gaps are closed in this build. The rest are named here so they are decisions, not surprises.

#FindingWhat was doneState
1

A brand-new user's Today had no primary button: nothing waits yet, so the page had three equal "Prepare application" buttons and no clear first move.

When nothing waits, the first pick's button becomes the primary one.

In this build
2

The first result takes about 45 seconds. The server will not save the last onboarding step until matching has finished, so the wait cannot be moved behind Today from the web.

The real phases are shown and the user lands on Today as soon as the stream ends. Removing the wait needs a small server change: accept the confirm step while matching continues.

Open · server
3

Two ways to apply remain on a job: "Prepare application" and "Apply on company site". They mark a job applied by different signals.

Prepare is the primary action; Apply on company site stays second for a user who wants to go now. One signal for "applied" is a follow-up.

Partly
4

The Assistant cannot run the loop. It can search, propose filter changes, tailor and write, but it has no tool to add a job to the list or prepare it.

Today's Ask box opens the Assistant as it is. Two new tools (add to list, prepare) would let a sentence do what the button does.

Open · server
5

The cost confirmation adds a tap to every preparation.

Kept on purpose: the existing rule is that a cost is on screen before anything is spent.

By design
6

The five-step Ready to apply setup is no longer on the main path. A user can prepare an application without ever seeing it.

Checked in the server: adding to the list and preparing do not require the setup. Missing profile details surface as a card under "Waiting for you"; the answers and weekly settings stay reachable from Getting ready.

In this build
7

"Target companies" is a list inside the saved search, not a thing of its own. A company with no open posting cannot be followed, and a resume cannot be tailored to a company without a posting.

The goal line links to the existing search panel, where companies can be set. A first-class company target is a separate piece of work.

Open
8

The search can still be edited in four places (onboarding, Jobs, Settings, the Getting ready list).

Today and Jobs now point at the same panel. Folding the other two in is a follow-up.

Partly
9

Interview prep is the part competitors do not have, and on phones it moved into More.

Accepted for the bottom bar's five-slot limit. A card on Today when an application reaches an interview stage would bring it back at the right moment.

Open
10

Words drift: Today says "application", the Getting ready pages still say "kit" and "Ready to apply" in 350 translated strings.

New screens use "application" throughout. The older pages keep their strings until one copy pass renames them in all nine languages.

Open
11

GoApply keeps its long onboarding.

Its consent and identity steps are legal requirements. It gets Today, the new navigation, and the same Jobs and Applications changes.

By design
12

The weekly list could be prepared with no tap at all.

Not built: it spends credits without a confirmation. It needs a setting the user turns on and an owner decision on the wording.

Open
13

If the job search stream fails during onboarding, the same server rule as finding 2 means setup cannot be finished from the web.

The user is not stranded: they land on Today with setup paused and a "Finish setting up" card. Their chosen levels are not saved until they finish. Same server fix as finding 2.

Open · server
14

The first onboarding screen offers four ways to add a resume as equals, and choosing one still needs a second tap on Continue.

Found on the built screen. It reuses the existing resume step unchanged. Making upload the one emphasised door and moving on as soon as a resume is in would save a tap.

Open
15

A job already on the list still shows "Prepare application" on its card until tapped: the job list does not carry the list state.

The tap is safe. The server returns the existing application, nothing is prepared twice, and the button turns into "Being prepared" or "Check and open". The right label up front needs one more field from the server.

Partly
16

For an account without Ready to apply, loading Today reads the top of the job list, and that read clears the "new jobs" badge on Jobs.

The count on Today is read first, so its own line stays right. A read that does not count as a visit needs a small server endpoint.

Open · server
Open decisions

One decision is the owner's

Should the product ever click Submit?

This redesign keeps the standing ruling: the agents find, tailor, prepare and fill; the user submits. "Apply jobs for the user" was built up to that line and not past it.

Crossing it is possible but it is not a design change. It reverses a ruling confirmed in July and in October, needs server-side submission that does not exist today, changes the copy gate and the extension gate, and changes what the product may honestly say about itself.
